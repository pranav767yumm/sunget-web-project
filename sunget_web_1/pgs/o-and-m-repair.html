Why solar plants need looking after

Solar has no moving parts, but output still drops quietly over time. Most losses are small, hidden and fixable.

Dust and bird droppings

A film of dirt blocks sunlight. In dry months it can cut output noticeably between cleanings.

Hotspots and cracked cells

Damaged cells overheat and drag down the whole string. You cannot see them by eye.

Loose or corroded connections

MC4 connectors and cable joints loosen with heat and weather, causing losses and fire risk.

Inverter faults and shading

Tripped inverters and new shade from trees or buildings can go unnoticed for weeks.

Do you maintain plants that someone else installed?

Yes. We start with a baseline health check so we know the plant's condition and what needs fixing before we agree a contract.

How often should panels be cleaned?

It depends on dust, rain, nearby construction and bird activity. Most plants need cleaning every few weeks. We adjust the schedule once we see how quickly yours gets dirty.

Can cleaning damage my panels?

Not if it is done properly. We use soft brushes or wipes and clean water, and we avoid cleaning in the hottest part of the day, since cold water on hot glass can stress it.

How do you know my plant is underperforming?

We compare actual generation with the expected output for your plant and weather, then trace gaps with string tests and thermal images.

What if a panel or inverter is under warranty?

We diagnose the fault, collect the evidence the manufacturer asks for and help you raise the claim. Repairs outside warranty are quoted before work begins.

Is a maintenance contract compulsory?

No. You can book a one-time health check or a repair visit. A contract simply gives you scheduled visits and faster response.
