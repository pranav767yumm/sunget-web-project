ETC or FPC: which one suits you

Both heat water with sunlight. They differ in how they are built and where they work best.

Evacuated Tube Collector (ETC)

A bank of glass vacuum tubes holds heat well, even on cool or cloudy days. It is the most common choice for homes and is lower in cost.

Best for: homes, small societies, hostels and places with hard-water-free supply.

Flat Plate Collector (FPC)

A flat, glass-covered panel with copper tubes inside. It is sturdier, handles hard water and pressure better and lasts longer.

Best for: hotels, hospitals, industries and areas with hard water.

Solar with electric or heat-pump backup

Solar does the heavy lifting. On long rainy spells an electric element or a heat pump tops up the temperature so you never run out of hot water.

Best for: commercial buildings and homes in the monsoon-heavy months.

Questions about solar water heaters

Not here? Call +91 98818 55770 and we will answer.

Will I get hot water on cloudy or rainy days?

You will still get warm water, especially with ETC. For long rainy spells we recommend an electric or heat-pump backup so the temperature stays steady.

How much roof space does it need?

A 100 litre per day system needs roughly 25–30 sq ft of shadow-free space. Larger systems scale from there.

Can it connect to my existing bathrooms?

Yes. We connect the solar tank to your existing hot water lines. For tall buildings we design the piping and a circulation loop so hot water reaches every floor.

Does hard water damage it?

Hard water can cause scale in ETC systems. If your water is hard, we suggest FPC or a water softener, and we offer descaling service.

How long does a solar water heater last?

A well-maintained system commonly runs for 15–20 years. Tubes, valves and the backup element may need replacing along the way.

Is there any subsidy or tax benefit?

Benefits for solar thermal systems depend on state and central schemes and on whether you are a home or business. We check what is currently available when we quote.
