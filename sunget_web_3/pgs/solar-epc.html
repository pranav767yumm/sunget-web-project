What turnkey EPC covers

EPC stands for Engineering, Procurement and Construction. We take responsibility for all three, and for the approvals that follow.

Engineering

Roof and structure assessment, shadow analysis, system design, electrical drawings and generation estimates.

Procurement

Panels, inverters, structures, cables and protection gear sourced from tested, certified suppliers.

Construction

Civil work, mounting, wiring, installation, testing and commissioning by trained crews under site supervision.

Approvals

DISCOM application, inspection, grid synchronisation and net-metering, filed and followed up by us.

Our EPC process, step by step

A clear sequence with a named owner at each stage, so nothing falls between contractors.

  1. Survey and feasibility

    Site visit, roof or land check, load study and a first estimate of size and savings.

  2. Design and proposal

    Structural and electrical design, bill of materials, fixed price and payback report.

  3. Approvals

    DISCOM application, net-metering request and any statutory permissions.

  4. Procurement

    Equipment ordered, inspected on arrival and delivered to site in sequence.

  5. Construction

    Mounting, wiring, inverter and protection installation, then testing.

  6. Grid sync and handover

    Inspection, meter installation, synchronisation, training and documents handed over.

Choose how you want to pay

Own the plant outright, or pay only for the power it produces.

Capex (you own the plant)

You pay for the plant and own it from day one. This gives the highest long-term savings and may qualify for tax benefits such as accelerated depreciation.

  • Highest lifetime savings
  • Plant is your asset
  • Upfront investment required

Opex (pay per unit)

We set up and maintain the plant. You pay a fixed rate per unit of solar power, usually lower than your grid tariff, with no upfront cost.

  • No upfront investment
  • Immediate bill reduction
  • Maintenance handled by us

What is the difference between EPC and just buying panels?

An EPC partner is responsible for the finished, working plant. That includes design, equipment, installation, testing and approvals. With separate vendors, you manage the gaps between them yourself.

Will my roof take the weight?

We check the roof type, age and load capacity before design. If reinforcement is needed, we tell you before you commit and include it in the proposal.

Who handles the DISCOM and net-metering paperwork?

We do. We prepare the documents, submit the application, coordinate the inspection and follow up until the plant is synchronised and the net meter is installed.

Can the plant be monitored remotely?

Yes. Inverters come with online monitoring, so you can see generation and faults from your phone or computer. We can also monitor it for you under a maintenance plan.

What happens after commissioning?

You get a handover pack and operator training. We offer annual maintenance contracts covering cleaning, inspections, thermal imaging and repairs.

Do you build ground-mount plants?

Yes. For ground-mount, our scope can include land survey, civil work, earthing, cabling and the connection to your transformer or HT supply. Share your land details and we will advise.
